### Lesson 999. Proper Care and Maintenance of the Computer

  1. Keep your computer clean by regularly dusting the keyboard, screen, and vents.
  1. Ensure proper ventilation to prevent overheating; avoid blocking air vents.
  1. Use a surge protector to protect against power surges and electrical damage.
  1. Install and update antivirus software to guard against malware and viruses.
  1. Regularly update your operating system and software for security and performance.
  1. Back up important data frequently to avoid data loss.
  1. Handle hardware components gently and avoid eating or drinking near your computer.
  1. Disconnect external devices safely to prevent data corruption.
  1. Store your computer in a dry, cool place away from direct sunlight and moisture.
  1. Schedule routine maintenance checks to ensure optimal performance.

### Lesson 999. Corporate Organization

	Note: These elements are typically found in a corporate organization. Here's a brief overview of each:

  1. Departments: These are specialized divisions within a company, each focusing on specific functions like marketing, finance, human resources, etc.
  1. Management: This refers to the group of individuals who run the company on a day-to-day basis, including executives and managers.
  1. Board of Directors: A group of individuals elected by shareholders to oversee the company's management and make major decisions.
  1. Committees: Sub-groups within the board or organization that focus on specific areas like audit, compensation, or governance.
  1. Shareholders: The owners of the company, who hold shares of stock and have voting rights on major company decisions.

	End Note: Together, these components form the structure of a corporate organization, ensuring it operates efficiently and effectively.

## Hierarchical Level of Corporate Organization

### Lesson 999. Conceptual Skills

  1. Ability to visualize (see) the organization as a whole
  1. Includes analytical, creative, and initiative skills
  1. Ability to identify the causes of the problems not the symptoms
  1. Ability to solve problems for the benefit of the entire organization
  1. Ability to fix goals for the whole organization and to plan for every situation
  1. Top-level managers spend more time in planning, organizing, and problem-solving
  
### Lesson 999. Human Relation Skills

  1. It is also called Interpersonal Skills
  1. Ability to understand, communicate and work with others
  1. It also helps the managers to lead, motivate, and develop team spirit
  1. It is required by all managers at all levels of management
 
### Lesson 999. Technical Skills

  1. Ability to perform a given job
  1. Ability to use different machines and tools
  1. Ability to use various procedures and techniques
  1. Low-level managers require more technical skills because they are in-charge of the actual operations

### Lesson 999. Communication Skills

  1. It is a required skill to all three levels of management
  1. Ability to communicated the plans and policies to the workers
  1. Ability to listen and solve the problems of the workers
  1. Ability to encourage a free-flow of communication in the organization

### Lesson 999. Administrative Skills

  1. It is required at the top-level management
  1. Ability to make plans and policies
  1. Ability to know how to get the work done
  1. Ability to co-ordinate different activities of the organizations
  1. Ability to control the full organization

### Lesson 999. Leadership Skills

  1. Ability to influence human behavior
  1. Ability to motivate others
  1. Ability to get the work done through the workers

### Lesson 999. Problem Solving Skills

  1. It is also called design skills
  1. Ability to identify a problem
  1. Ability to find the best solution for solving any specific problem
  1. It requires intelligence, experience, and up-to-date knowledge of the latest developments
  
### Lesson 999. Decision-Making Skills

  1. It is required at all levels of management
  1. It is more required at the top-level of management
  1. Ability to take quick and correct decisions
  1. Ability to implement the decision wisely
  1. The success or failure of a manager depends upon the correctness of his decisions

### Lesson 999. Elements of Good Feasibility Study

  1. Clear Objectives: A good feasibility study should have well-defined objectives that align with the project's goals.
  1. Comprehensive Analysis: It should include a thorough analysis of all relevant factors, including technical, economic, legal, and operational aspects.
  1. Stakeholder Involvement: Engaging stakeholders throughout the process is crucial to gather diverse perspectives and ensure buy-in.
  1. Risk Assessment: Identifying potential risks and challenges is essential for developing effective mitigation strategies.
  1. Realistic Recommendations: The study should provide practical and achievable recommendations based on the analysis conducted.
